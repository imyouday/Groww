**CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)** measures the annualized return of a single lump sum investment over a period, assuming compounding. It ignores intermediate cash flows.

**XIRR (Extended Internal Rate of Return)** calculates the annualized return when there are multiple cash flows at irregular intervals (e.g., SIP, STP, SWP, partial redemptions). It solves for the rate that makes the present value of all cash flows equal to the current value.

For a one-time investment, CAGR = XIRR. For SIPs or any staggered investments, XIRR is the correct measure. Mutual fund factsheets typically show CAGR for standard periods (1Y, 3Y, 5Y). Your personal return (account statement) uses XIRR. Both are annualized percentages.
